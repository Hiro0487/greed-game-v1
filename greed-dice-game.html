    How the table plays

    Score & choose

    Roll five dice. After a scoring roll, choose at least one scoring die to set aside. You may leave other scoring dice in play. Set-aside dice stay locked for the turn.

    • Standard singles: 1 = 100; 5 = 50
    • Standard three 1s = 1,000; four = 2,000; five = 4,000
    • Standard three 2s–6s = face × 100; larger sets have their own values
    • Standard straights 1–2–3–4–5 and 2–3–4–5–6 = 1,000
    • Every value can be customized in Game Settings before the game starts.

    Combos

    A three-, four-, or five-of-a-kind uses its matching custom value instead of adding single-die points. You may break a straight into the scoring 1 and/or 5, but then you give up the straight. Combos only form within the current roll.

    Risk, safety & hot dice

    A roll with no score ends the turn. Ordinary turn points are lost, but the custom points earned by three, four, or five 1s and either straight are protected. Score all five dice cumulatively and all five return as hot dice for a fresh roll. House rules decide whether a qualified player may stop there.

    Optional timers

    A game limit ends play at zero and awards the win to the highest banked score; a tie goes to whoever reached that score first. A human who runs out of turn time automatically banks when qualified. An unqualified player loses ordinary turn points but keeps protected points from three, four, or five 1s and straights. Bots show the same countdown but keep playing at their normal pace.

    Qualify, bank & win

    Before entering the scoreboard, reach the threshold in a single turn. After qualifying, any valid turn can be banked. Points only win when banked. With Require exact score off, custom scoring values may exceed the target and banking at or above the target starts one final turn for every other player. With it on, custom values are capped at the target and a player must bank exactly the target; dice or combinations that would push the projected total over the target cannot be selected. After the last chance is taken, the game ends immediately—the first finisher does not play again. If more than one player reaches the required winning score, the last to do so wins.

    Online multiplayer is planned for a future version.
